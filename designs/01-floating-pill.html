The quiet return of the small, slow website

Marta Bexell · 9 min read · September 2026

Somewhere between the third cookie banner and the fourth newsletter modal, a certain kind of reader gave up. Not on reading — on the machinery around it. What has grown back in the gap is smaller and stranger than anyone expected.

The sites are hand-made and frequently ugly. They load in under a second on a train. They are maintained by one person who answers their own email. None of this is nostalgia; it is a straightforward response to a web that got heavier every year while getting no better at the only thing it was for.

What is genuinely new is the tooling underneath. It has never been cheaper to run something small and permanent, and it has never been easier to make it feel considered rather than merely cheap.
